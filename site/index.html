How I make decisions

Evidence-based management

Good decisions combine the best available research, your company’s data, and practical experience. I make assumptions explicit, measure what matters, and adjust when the evidence changes.

Be clear about what we know, what we assume, and what would change our minds. Act with the evidence available — then check the result.

Results, not anecdotes

A success story can suggest an idea, but it does not establish cause and effect. I look for measurable outcomes and evidence that applies to your situation.

Testable objectives

Before introducing a process or tool, we define the problem, the expected benefit, and how to assess it. A framework needs to earn its place.

Update the assumptions

Plans start with assumptions about costs, timelines, and behaviour. We make them visible and revise them as new information arrives.

Practical significance

A statistically significant result may still have little business value. We weigh the size of the benefit against cost, effort, uncertainty, and risk.

Test before scaling

Independent replication builds confidence. Where evidence is limited, a proportionate pilot and clear review criteria are better than an unqualified rollout.

Look for missing evidence

Published successes do not tell the whole story. I look for negative findings, conflicts of interest, and limits in the data before drawing conclusions.
