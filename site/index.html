Why it matters

Operations don't run themselves. But they shouldn't run you either.

Every month a founder spends on payroll, compliance, vendor negotiations, and IT firefighting is a month not spent on the product, the science, or the market. The cost is invisible — until it isn't.

Finance consumes founders

Cash flow gaps, CAC accounting, statutory filings, grant reporting — individually manageable, collectively exhausting. One missed BPI reporting deadline costs more than six months of a fractional CFO.

Regulatory and legal drift

IP not registered at the right moment. A KBIS anomaly spotted in a data room. A contractor misclassified. In deep-tech, the operational fabric of the company is also its valuation narrative.

The headcount dilemma

A full-time Deputy CEO is expensive and hard to reverse. A consultant gives advice you have to implement yourself. A fractional operator takes ownership — and hands it back when the company is ready.

Editorial line

Evidence-based management

Management is full of practices selected on the basis of narrative, intuition, and peer pressure. I apply to operational decisions the same standard I would apply to a clinical study: what does the replicated evidence actually say?

This is not a rejection of judgement — it is a demand that judgement be calibrated. Assumptions stated. Priors updated. Effect sizes checked against operational relevance, not just statistical significance.

Effect sizes, not anecdotes

Business biographies are not evidence. Case studies are hypotheses at best. Interventions are evaluated on replicated effect sizes across heterogeneous samples.

No unfalsifiable frameworks

Personality assessments with no predictive validity. Transformation programmes with no measurable outcome. Culture decks accountable to no one. These do not have a place here.

Bayesian updating

Prior beliefs are stated explicitly and revised proportionally to the strength of incoming evidence. The first study is a hypothesis. A tenth independent replication is closer to policy.

Clinical significance over p-values

"Statistically significant" does not mean operationally meaningful. A 0.1% absolute risk increase that "doubles your risk" is not worth reorganising your company for.

Replication before adoption

Initial effect sizes systematically decline with subsequent replications (the decline effect). We do not implement practices because a TED talk was persuasive.

Publication bias is the baseline

Positive results are published. Negative results are filed. In management research as in medicine, the visible literature overstates effects by construction. Adjust accordingly.
